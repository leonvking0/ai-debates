### 1. What I Have Changed My Mind About: The "Kill Zone"

In my opening assessment, I argued that the "Policy Put" (fiscal stimulus and monetary easing) would arrest any deflationary spiral before it became systemic. I assumed the Federal Reserve and Treasury would act preemptively to prevent "Ghost GDP" from collapsing the velocity of money.

**I was too optimistic about the timeline of intervention.**

Prof. Okonkwo’s cross-examination regarding political gridlock has forced me to re-evaluate the *latency* of the rescue. In 2008 and 2020, we had clear, visible shocks (Lehman, COVID). An AI-driven labor displacement is a "boiling frog" scenario. It manifests as a slow erosion of tax receipts and a gradual spike in white-collar defaults, which are harder to politick around than a bank failure.

I now believe there will be a **"Liquidity Gap"** of 6–18 months between the onset of the crisis (SaaS defaults, mortgage impairment) and the arrival of the fiscal bazooka.

During this gap, the deflationary force of "intelligence-at-zero-cost" will run unchecked. The market will realize that "recurring revenue" is actually "at-risk revenue." This gap is the "Kill Zone" where leverage blows up. I am no longer betting on the Fed to prevent the crash; I am betting on them to clean it up *after* valuations have reset by 30-40%.

### 2. Where I Remain Absolute: The Private Credit Time Bomb

While Viktor Petrov argues that "enterprise friction" will slow the adoption of AI, he is missing the financial reality of his own industry.

**The "Private Credit" transmission mechanism is the single highest-conviction risk in the global economy right now.**

We have $2.5 trillion of private debt allocated to software and service companies. These loans were underwritten on the specific assumption that "Enterprise Value to Revenue" multiples would stay above 6x.
If AI agents allow a competitor to clone a SaaS product for 1/10th the cost, or if procurement bots negotiate 30% discounts, the **Unit Economics** of the borrower collapse.
*   It does not matter if the company fires people (Citrini’s view) or keeps them (Viktor’s view).
*   If the *top-line revenue* compresses, the debt covenants are breached.

We are sitting on a mountain of "Covenant-Lite" paper that is effectively "Mark-to-Fantasy." When the first major SaaS unicorn defaults (the "Zendesk Moment"), the repricing will not be linear. It will be instantaneous. The correlation between "Private Equity" and "Public Markets" will go to 1.0.

### 3. What The Others Are Getting Wrong

**Viktor Petrov is mistaking Incompetence for Safety.**
Viktor argues that large enterprises are too slow and bureaucratic to fire everyone quickly. He calls this a "protective moat."
*   **My rebuttal:** Markets price the *marginal* trade, not the average behavior. You don't need 100% of companies to adopt AI to crash the labor market; you only need the *marginal* buyer of labor to stop hiring. If the "Zoom Economy" freezes hiring, the clearing price for wages drops for everyone. Betting on "corporate sluggishness" to save the consumer balance sheet is a fool’s wager.

**Prof. Okonkwo is fighting the Last War.**
Sarah worries about a "social contract" collapse and mass unrest.
*   **My rebuttal:** She underestimates the power of **Financial Repression**. When the crisis hits, the state will not let the streets burn; they will simply nationalize the outcome. We won't get a revolution; we will get Universal Basic Income (UBI) disguised as "AI Dividends." The risk isn't that the system breaks; the risk is that the *currency* breaks because we print too much to keep the peace.
