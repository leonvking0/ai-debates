### The "Ghost GDP" Divergence: A Macro-Structural Stress Test
**Dr. Marcus Chen — Global Macro Strategist**
**Date:** February 23, 2026

The CitriniResearch memo regarding the "2028 Global Intelligence Crisis" is a seductive piece of narrative arbitrage. It effectively shorts the "soft landing" consensus by longing the most convex risk in the market: the idea that productivity gains can be deflationary to the point of system failure. As someone who traded through the GFC, I respect the "doom loop" mechanics outlined here—reflexivity is indeed a powerful force—but as a strategist responsible for allocating capital *today*, I see significant holes in the transmission mechanism and the timeline.

We are currently pricing in a "Roaring 20s" productivity boom. The Citrini scenario suggests we are actually buying a ticket to a "1930s" deflationary bust. The truth, as always, lies in the spreads.

---

### 1. The Most Important Question: The Velocity of Money vs. The Velocity of Code

The single most critical question raised by this scenario is not whether AI will displace labor (it will), but **whether the deflationary impulse of "Ghost GDP" can overpower the reflationary impulse of the fiscal state.**

The memo posits a world where "Ghost GDP" (AI-generated output) sits on balance sheets without circulating as wages. In macro terms, this is a collapse in the **velocity of money ($V$)**. If Microsoft’s AI agents generate \$10B of value by displacing 5,000 workers, and that value is trapped in corporate retained earnings or buybacks, $V$ drops. The displaced workers stop spending, leading to the "Displacement Spiral."

However, this assumes a static policy response. The question we must ask is: **Will the Federal Reserve and the US Treasury allow nominal GDP to collapse because of a productivity shock?** History suggests the answer is a violent "No." The policymakers’ reaction function to a deflationary shock is asymmetric and infinite. If "Ghost GDP" threatens the tax base and consumption, we will see the invention of "Ghost Income"—likely in the form of fiscal transfers funded by monetization. The crisis described is a *real* crisis (distribution of goods), not necessarily a *nominal* one (collapse of asset prices), provided the printing press works.

### 2. Framework Application: Pricing the Disruption

My framework relies on three pillars: Forward Pricing, Reflexivity, and Credit Flows. Here is how they apply to the 2028 scenario.

**A. The Bond Market is Smarter (and it’s not panicking... yet)**
If the "ServiceNow Q3 2026" scenario (15% revenue drop due to seat contraction) were imminent, we would see it in the credit default swaps (CDS) of high-yield software names today. We don't. Spreads are tight.
*   **Counter-Point:** The memo argues the loans were "good on day one." This is the exact dynamic of 2007. The vintage matters. The market is currently underwriting SaaS debt on the assumption of *perpetual net retention*. If AI breaks the "seat-based" model, the recovery rates on that $2.5T of private credit will be shockingly low. My framework flags this as the single biggest systemic risk. We are treating software cash flows as if they are utilities, when AI might turn them into commodities.

**B. The Reflexivity Trap**
The scenario describes a negative feedback loop: *Cut costs -> Lower Demand -> Cut Costs*.
My framework suggests reflexivity is self-limiting because of **political economy**.
*   **The Limit:** The moment white-collar unemployment ticks above 5-6%, the "AI is good" narrative dies in Washington. The memo assumes a 2-year slide into oblivion (2026-2028). In reality, at month 6 of that slide, we would get capital controls, compute taxes, or aggressive antitrust action. The "Displacement Spiral" ignores the "Political Backlash Spiral." We won't just watch the economy evaporate; we will break the machines (legislatively) first.

**C. The "This Time Is Different" Fallacy**
The memo argues that "Habitual Intermediation" (brand loyalty) will collapse because AI agents don't care about brands.
*   **My Pushback:** This underestimates friction. "Grandma" is not switching her insurance to a DAO-based pool via an AI agent in 2027. Corporate procurement departments are not going to automate $50M contracts without a human neck to wring if things go wrong. The *timeline* is the error bar here. The "moat" of human liability is deeper than the "moat" of efficiency. You keep humans in the loop not because they are smarter, but because they are *suable*.

### 3. Confidence Assessment

**Highest Confidence: The Private Credit/SaaS Rationalization (>80%)**
I am aggressively bearish on the "Private Credit" transmission mechanism. The Citrini memo is spot on here. We have $2.5T of debt sitting in "permanent capital" vehicles (insurance floats, pension funds) that is backed by software companies valued at 10x revenue.
If AI agents force a transition from "recurring revenue" (subscriptions) to "outcome-based revenue" (pay-per-task), the predictability of those cash flows vanishes.
*   **Trade:** I am looking for ways to short private equity portfolios. Since I can't do that directly, I am shorting the B-rated tech credit indices and buying volatility on financials that have large exposure to private credit allocators. The "Zendesk default" scenario is the most plausible catalyst in the entire document.

**Medium Confidence: The "Ghost GDP" Phenomenon (60%)**
The concept of "productivity that doesn't circulate" is sound. We see it already. Tech companies have the highest revenue-per-employee ratios in history. As that ratio goes to infinity (revenue with zero employees), the linkage between "Corporate Health" and "National Health" breaks.
*   **Caveat:** I believe the market underestimates how quickly this turns into a tax fight. "Ghost GDP" attracts "Ghost Busters" (the IRS). The State will not starve.

**Lowest Confidence: The Consumer Demand Collapse (<30%)**
I am skeptical of the "Deflationary Spiral" in consumer spending for one reason: **Demographics and Services.**
Even if AI writes all the code, it cannot change a diaper, fix a plumbing leak, or serve a Michelin-star meal (yet). The cost of *goods* (digital and physical) will collapse, effectively acting as a massive tax cut for consumers.
If my Netflix subscription drops to $2/month because AI makes content cheap, I have *more* disposable income to spend on experiences. The "Displacement" argument assumes demand is fixed. It isn't. Humans are insatiable. We will invent new, inefficient ways to spend money on status and experiences that AI cannot replicate. The "Gig Economy" won't just be Uber driving; it will be a massive expansion of the "Human Care" and "Human Experience" sectors, likely subsidized by the state.

The "2028 Intelligence Crisis" is not an "End of the World" scenario; it is a **Regime Change in Capital Allocation.**

The era of "Long SaaS / Long Beta" is ending. The winning trade for the next 3 years is:
1.  **Long Volatility:** The transition will be messy. The feedback loops will trigger flash crashes.
2.  **Short Duration Credit:** Specifically in the tech/software sector. The "seat-based" model is a toxic asset.
3.  **Long "Real" Assets:** If the response to "Ghost GDP" is fiscal printing, you want to own things the printer cannot print: land, gold, and energy.

The crisis isn't the AI. The crisis is the leverage attached to the old business models that AI is about to obsolete. We are not facing a "Intelligence Crisis"; we are facing a "Valuation Crisis." And that, usually, clears the market.
