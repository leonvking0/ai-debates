# Block 4: The Geopolitical Dimension

**How does this play out differently across nations and systems?**

15. The US, China, and Europe have fundamentally different social contracts, labor protections, and political systems. How does each system absorb AI-driven labor displacement differently? Which system is most resilient, and which is most fragile?

16. China has 300+ million manufacturing and service workers whose cognitive tasks are AI-vulnerable, plus a social stability model built on economic growth delivering upward mobility. What happens to CCP legitimacy if this implicit contract breaks? Is China's ability to implement top-down redistribution an advantage or a liability?

17. Developing nations that were counting on demographic dividends and service-sector growth (India, Southeast Asia, Africa) — does AI permanently close the industrialization ladder that lifted East Asia? Could this create a new form of colonial-era dependency, where cognitive labor is extracted by AI-owning nations?

18. If capital becomes more important than labor, and capital is mobile while people are not, does this accelerate a "race to the bottom" on capital taxation? Or does the political pressure for redistribution create a "race to the top" on wealth taxes? Who wins this tug of war?

19. Could AI-driven economic disruption trigger actual geopolitical conflict? Historically, periods of rapid economic restructuring and rising inequality have preceded wars. What are the flashpoints?
