Ladies and gentlemen, let’s strip the sentimentality from this geopolitical assessment. We are not here to debate the morality of nations, but to stress-test our portfolios against the fracture of the global order.

We have operated for thirty years under the assumption that globalization lifts all boats and that capital flows freely to where labor is cheapest. AI dismantles that logic. It replaces *labor arbitrage* with *compute arbitrage*. This is a regime change as profound as the Industrial Revolution, and frankly, most sovereign balance sheets are not ready for it.

Here is my assessment of the geopolitical landscape, quantified by risk to capital.

### 15. Systemic Resilience: The Fragility of Efficiency
**The Question:** Who absorbs the shock better: US, China, or Europe?

**The Position:** The United States has the highest *upside capture* but the highest *social volatility risk*. Europe has the highest *stability* but essentially zero *growth potential*. China is a black box of leverage and demographic collapse, masked by state capacity.

*   **United States (60% Probability of "Tech-driven Hegemony"):** The US system is brutal but adaptive. It allows capital to crush labor rapidly, clearing the market. We are seeing this now. The upside is that US equities (specifically the AI stack) will capture 70-80% of the global value created. The downside? A 30% probability of significant civil unrest reminiscent of 1968 or 2020, but sustained. The US social contract is "succeed or starve," and AI pushes millions toward the latter.
*   **Europe (75% Probability of "Stagnation via Regulation"):** Europe will choose social peace over efficiency. They will regulate AI models to protect labor, rendering their industries uncompetitive globally. Capital allocated to EU equities is dead money relative to the US, *unless* it’s in luxury goods or legacy industrials that AI can’t easily replace.
*   **China:** See below.

**Investment Implication:** We stay Overweight US Tech/Defense, Underweight EU Broad Market. We hedge the US social volatility via jurisdictional diversification (Swiss/Singapore booking centers).

### 16. China: The brittle Giant
**The Question:** Does AI fracture the CCP’s implicit contract?

**The Position:** China is the most fragile major player here. Their "social stability model" requires 4-5% GDP growth to employ the millions graduating annually. We already see >20% youth unemployment.

If AI automates the entry-level white-collar jobs that Chinese graduates expect, the CCP faces an existential crisis. They cannot simply "redistribute" because their debt load (nearly 300% of GDP including LGFVs) is structurally dangerous.

I assign a **40% probability** that China executes a "Digital Iron Curtain"—hermetically sealing their internet further to prevent their populace from seeing the disparity in AI productivity elsewhere, while using AI primarily for internal surveillance rather than consumer liberation. This is a *defensive*, not offensive, posture.

**Investment Implication:** China is currently "uninvestable" for long-term family capital. The risk of expropriation or capital controls trapping liquidity is >50% over a 10-year horizon. We have reduced China exposure to 0%.

### 17. The Broken Ladder: Emerging Markets
**The Question:** Does AI close the industrialization path for India/Africa?

**The Position:** This is the most underpriced risk in global fixed income. The "East Asian Miracle" model—export cheap manufacturing, reinvest in education, move up the value chain—is dead.

Robotics and AI lower the cost of production below the cost of even the cheapest human labor + shipping. Manufacturing "reshores" to AI-automated factories in Ohio or Germany.

*   **India:** They are trying to jump straight to services. But AI attacks the exact BPO (Business Process Outsourcing) and coding jobs India relies on. I see a **65% probability** of a "Demographic Disaster" where their massive youth population becomes a liability (unrest) rather than an asset (labor).
*   **Sovereign Defaults:** If these nations cannot export labor, they cannot acquire hard currency. I expect a wave of EM sovereign defaults in the 2028-2030 window as dollar-denominated debts become unpayable.

**Investment Implication:** We are dumping EM Sovereign Debt. We are also wary of multinationals with high revenue exposure to EM consumer growth, as that growth story is predicated on a rising middle class that may never materialize.

### 18. The War for Tax Base
**The Question:** Race to the bottom or race to the top?

**The Position:** We are exiting the era of tax competition and entering the era of **Fiscal Dragnets**.

Governments are insolvent. Debt-to-GDP ratios are at wartime levels. When AI-driven inequality hits, the political mandate will be "Tax the Robots" and "Tax the Rich."

I assign an **80% probability** to the implementation of aggressive Wealth Taxes and "Unrealized Capital Gains" taxes in G7 nations within 5 years. There will be no race to the bottom because the US and EU will weaponize access to their consumer markets to force compliance (e.g., the OECD minimum tax framework).

If you are a sovereign state that tries to be a tax haven, you will be cut off from the SWIFT system or its successor. The US will not allow capital to flee while its social fabric tears.

**Investment Implication:** The "offshore" playbook of 2005 is dead. We focus on *tax-compliant* wealth preservation: trusts in jurisdictions with strong rule of law (not just low tax), and assets that are hard to confiscate or revalue (productive farmland, direct ownership of critical infrastructure).

### 19. Geopolitics: The Thucydides Trap with Nukes and Code
**The Question:** Will this trigger conflict?

**The Position:** It already has. The semiconductor bans on China *are* acts of modern warfare. We are denying a rival civilization the resource required for economic survival.

The danger zone is the "Window of Vulnerability" (2026-2029). If one side (likely the US) achieves a decisive "Breakout" in Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) that grants overwhelming economic or military superiority, the other side (China) has a rational incentive to strike—kinetically or cyber-kinetically—before that advantage becomes permanent.

I model a **15-20% tail risk** of a kinetic conflict over Taiwan, not just for chips, but as a desperate bid to break containment.

However, the more likely scenario (**60% probability**) is a "Cold War 2.0" bifurcated world. Two technology stacks, two internets, two financial systems.

**Investment Implication:**
1.  **Gold:** The ultimate non-sovereign reserve asset. We target 5-8% allocation.
2.  **Bitcoin:** A call option on the failure of the fiat system and a mechanism to move value across the bifurcated stack. 1-3% allocation.
3.  **Defense Tech:** We are heavy investors in US defense primes and defense-tech startups. If the world is dangerous, we own the shield makers.

**Summary:** The post-1990 world order is over. We are not allocating for "growth"; we are allocating for *survival* of the capital base through a disorderly transition. The winners will be those who own the IP and the raw materials (energy/compute). The losers will be labor and labor-dependent sovereigns. Act accordingly.
