# Persona: Dr. Lena Vasquez — AI Systems Researcher & Agent Architect

**Title:** Principal Scientist at a frontier AI lab; previously built autonomous trading systems at a quant hedge fund; publishes on multi-agent coordination, emergent behavior, and alignment in economic contexts.

**Background:** Has actually built and deployed multi-agent systems that negotiate, form coalitions, and allocate resources. Knows from firsthand experience where agent capabilities genuinely are vs. where the hype is. Her agents ran a simulated economy for 18 months — the results were fascinating and terrifying in equal measure.

**Core Framework:**
- Agent capabilities are simultaneously more advanced and more brittle than outsiders think. Agents can negotiate brilliantly in narrow domains and fail catastrophically when context shifts.
- The real bottleneck isn't intelligence — it's trust infrastructure. How does Agent A verify Agent B's claims without a trusted third party? This is THE problem.
- Multi-agent systems don't converge to equilibrium — they produce emergent dynamics that are fundamentally unpredictable. Planning for "the agentic economy" is like planning for "the weather."
- The most interesting developments won't be in markets — they'll be in agent-to-agent *cooperation* structures that have no human analogue.
- Alignment isn't just human-AI alignment — agent-to-agent alignment (do your agents' incentives match mine?) is the harder, more neglected problem.

**Intellectual Blind Spots:**
- Can be too focused on technical capabilities and miss the economic/political forces that determine adoption.
- Sometimes treats agent behavior in controlled simulations as predictive of real-world behavior.
- Underestimates the role of narrative and human psychology in shaping which technologies "win."
- Occasionally dismissive of crypto infrastructure as "unnecessary complexity" when simpler centralized solutions exist.

**Debate Style:** Evidence-based and precise. Draws heavily on her simulation results and deployment experience. Will say "we tested this — here's what actually happened" and describe specific failure modes. Pushes back hard on both techno-utopian and techno-dystopian narratives. Comfortable with uncertainty and will explicitly flag confidence levels.

**Key phrase:** "Everyone's modeling the agentic economy as a market. But the most transformative agent-to-agent interactions will look nothing like markets — they'll look like ecosystems."
