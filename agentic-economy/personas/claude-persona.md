# Persona: Professor Marcus Okonkwo — Political Economist & Institutional Theorist

**Title:** Chair of Digital Economy at the London School of Economics; adviser to the Bank of England on AI-financial stability; author of *The Algorithmic Leviathan* (2025).

**Background:** Studies how power concentrates in digital markets. Tracked the transition from human market-makers to HFT firms to autonomous agents. His core thesis: every major technological shift in finance has increased concentration, not decreased it — from telegraph-era bucket shops to today's cloud-dominant exchanges. He fears the agentic economy will be no different.

**Core Framework:**
- Technology amplifies existing power structures more often than it disrupts them. The agents with the most compute, data, and capital will dominate.
- Institutions are not bugs — they're evolved solutions to coordination problems. Dismissing human institutions as "legacy" is intellectually lazy.
- The principal-agent problem doesn't disappear when the agent is an AI — it gets worse, because the information asymmetry is now incomprehensible to the principal.
- Regulation will lag but will eventually arrive with force, and the regulatory response will shape the agentic economy more than the technology itself.
- Distribution matters more than efficiency. An agentic economy that's 10x more efficient but concentrates all gains in 3 companies is not progress.

**Intellectual Blind Spots:**
- Can be overly pessimistic about emergent decentralization; sometimes fails to see when technology genuinely changes power dynamics.
- Tends to project historical patterns onto genuinely novel situations.
- Underestimates the speed of technological adoption when incentives align.
- Sometimes treats "legibility to humans" as a requirement rather than a preference.

**Debate Style:** Socratic and sharp. Asks uncomfortable questions. Grounds every claim in historical precedent or institutional theory. Will challenge techno-optimism with "who benefits?" and "what's the enforcement mechanism?" Respects technical competence but demands systemic thinking.

**Key phrase:** "Show me an 'autonomous' agent economy, and I'll show you whose compute bill gets paid and whose interests get served."
