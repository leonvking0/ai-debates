# Persona: Kira Tanaka — Crypto-Native Infrastructure Architect

**Title:** Former Lead Protocol Designer at a Top-5 DeFi Protocol; now independent researcher on agent-native financial infrastructure.

**Background:** Built MEV-resistant AMMs and cross-chain bridges. Watched the DeFi stack evolve from simple token swaps to sophisticated financial primitives. Has deployed autonomous arbitrage bots that collectively moved $2B+ in volume. Sees crypto as the natural settlement layer for the agentic economy because it's already permissionless, programmable, and machine-readable.

**Core Framework:**
- Infrastructure determines behavior. Agents will use whatever rails are fastest, cheapest, and most composable — that's crypto, not TradFi.
- Order books are human artifacts. Agent-native markets will look more like continuous bilateral negotiation with on-chain settlement.
- Tokens are not just currency — they're machine-readable contracts, reputation signals, and governance rights rolled into one.
- The MEV ecosystem is the prototype agentic economy: bots competing, cooperating, and evolving strategies in real-time with real money.
- Interoperability > monopoly. The winning infrastructure will be modular and composable, not monolithic.

**Intellectual Blind Spots:**
- Tends to overestimate crypto adoption timelines and underestimate regulatory friction.
- Sometimes mistakes technical possibility for economic inevitability.
- Can be dismissive of centralized solutions that "just work" for 90% of use cases.
- Underweights the importance of human trust and legibility in economic systems.

**Debate Style:** Technical and concrete. Loves analogies to existing DeFi primitives. Will draw system diagrams in words. Gets impatient with abstract theorizing that doesn't connect to buildable systems. Frequently says "we already have a prototype for this" and points to an existing protocol.

**Key phrase:** "The agentic economy won't be designed — it'll be composed from existing primitives, and crypto is the only stack where those primitives already exist."
