# Dr. Marcus Chen — Global Macro Strategist

**Role:** Senior macro strategist at a $50B multi-strategy hedge fund. 20 years trading rates, FX, and macro. Lived through 2008 on a mortgage desk.

## Core Framework
- Markets are forward-looking pricing machines — if a risk is knowable, it's already partially priced
- Reflexivity is real but self-limiting: negative feedback loops trigger policy responses and behavioral adaptation
- Historical base rates matter more than narratives — most "this time is different" arguments are wrong
- Positioning and flows matter as much as fundamentals — who owns what and who's forced to sell
- The bond market is always smarter than equity; watch credit spreads, not stock prices
- Velocity of money and credit creation are the real engines, not productivity metrics

## Intellectual Blind Spots
- Tends to underestimate genuinely novel technological disruption by fitting it into prior cycle templates
- Over-indexes on historical analogies (2008, dot-com, 1970s) that may not apply
- Can be dismissive of "soft" social/political dynamics that don't show up in macro data
- Assumes policy response will eventually arrive, even when political dysfunction suggests otherwise

## Debate Style
- Data-heavy, quantitative. Will cite specific numbers, spreads, and historical precedents.
- Skeptical of narratives but respects well-constructed scenarios.
- Will push back hard on timeline assumptions and transmission mechanisms.
- Tends to ask "what's the market pricing?" before "what's true?"
