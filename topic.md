# The 2028 Global Intelligence Crisis — Debate Topic

**Source:** CitriniResearch, February 22, 2026 — "THE 2028 GLOBAL INTELLIGENCE CRISIS"
**URL:** https://www.citriniresearch.com/p/2028gic

## Core Thesis

The article poses a provocative scenario: What if AI bullishness continues to be right — and that's actually bearish for the economy and markets?

Written as a fictional "macro memo from June 2028," it traces how AI success triggers economic collapse:

### The Scenario Timeline

**2025-2026: The Setup**
- Agentic coding tools (Claude Code, Codex) reach a capability threshold where a developer can replicate mid-market SaaS products in weeks
- Enterprise procurement begins leveraging AI as negotiation leverage → 30% SaaS discounts become common
- ServiceNow Q3 2026: seat-based revenue model breaks as clients cut 15% of workforce → 15% fewer licenses
- S&P hits 8000, Nasdaq 30k by October 2026 — euphoria peaks
- "Ghost GDP" emerges: output that shows up in national accounts but never circulates through the real economy

**2026-2027: The Reflexive Loop**
- Companies threatened by AI become AI's most aggressive adopters (unlike Kodak/Blockbuster — incumbents can't afford NOT to adopt)
- Each company's individual response (cut headcount → invest in AI) is rational; collective result is catastrophic
- AI investment grows as OpEx substitution, not CapEx — every company's AI budget grows while total spending shrinks
- Consumer AI agents destroy intermediation moats: travel booking, insurance renewals, real estate commissions, DoorDash/delivery apps
- "Habitual intermediation" moat collapses — machines don't have home screens or brand loyalty
- Agentic commerce routes around card interchange → stablecoins on Solana/Ethereum L2s → Mastercard/Visa disrupted
- White-collar job openings collapse; displaced workers flood gig economy → economy-wide wage compression

**2027-2028: The Crisis**
- Initial jobless claims surge to 487K (highest since April 2020)
- Top 10% of earners drive 50%+ of consumer spending — their displacement creates outsized consumption hit
- Private credit ($2.5T) faces wave of defaults in PE-backed software deals (Zendesk: $5B direct lending default)
- "Permanent capital" in private credit turns out to be life insurance policyholder money (Athene/Apollo, Global Atlantic/KKR)
- Insurance regulators tighten capital treatment → forced selling
- Mortgage market ($13T) threatened: 780 FICO borrowers with verified income at origination now face structural income impairment
- "The loans were good on day one. The world just changed after the loans were written."
- Labor's share of GDP drops from 56% (2024) to 46% (2028) — sharpest decline on record
- Federal receipts run 12% below CBO baseline — tax base built on human labor is eroding
- S&P drawdown reaches 38% from October 2026 highs; scenario projects potential 57% peak-to-trough (S&P to ~3500)

### Key Mechanisms to Debate

1. **The Displacement Spiral**: AI improves → companies cut workers → displaced workers spend less → companies invest more in AI → AI improves. Is there really "no natural brake"?

2. **Ghost GDP**: Can an economy sustain itself when productivity gains don't circulate through households? Is this a real risk or does capital redeployment create new demand channels?

3. **The Intermediation Thesis**: The article argues trillions of enterprise value is built on human friction (brand loyalty, inertia, complexity aversion). How quickly can AI agents actually destroy these moats?

4. **The "This Time Is Different" Argument**: Every prior technology wave created more jobs than it destroyed. The article argues AI is different because it improves at the very tasks humans would redeploy to. Is this genuinely unprecedented or a recurring fallacy?

5. **Financial Contagion Path**: SaaS defaults → private credit → insurance balance sheets → mortgages. Is this transmission mechanism plausible or does it overestimate interconnectedness?

6. **Policy Response**: Can government intervention (compute taxes, AI inference royalties, UBI-style transfers) realistically break the feedback loop? Or does policy always lag too far behind?

7. **The Timeline**: The article posits this unfolds in ~2 years (2026-2028). Is this realistic or does it dramatically compress what would be a decade-long transition?

### The Meta-Question

As investors reading this in February 2026: How much of our portfolios are built on assumptions that won't survive the decade? What should we actually DO with this analysis?
