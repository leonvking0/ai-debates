## Position

**SELL**. My opening target is **$85**.

This is a rate-sensitive balance-sheet business being priced like a software platform. If over **95% of revenue** is reserve yield on Treasuries, then the equity should not command a ~$28B-$32B valuation just because management wraps it in stablecoin infrastructure language. At roughly **double-digit trailing sales**, with Fed cuts compressing the core earnings engine, I see the market still underwriting a narrative that the numbers do not support.

## Key Claims
1. [claim-1] Circle is materially **overvalued** because the market is assigning fintech/software multiples to what is functionally a regulated spread business; with ~$1.7B revenue and a ~$30B market cap, investors are paying an absurd multiple for income that is mostly driven by short-term rates, not durable pricing power.
2. [claim-2] The **macro setup is deteriorating**, not improving: lower Fed rates directly squeeze reserve income, crypto-cycle enthusiasm is cooling from post-IPO euphoria, and Circle’s headline growth was flattered by a prior high-yield environment that is unlikely to persist into a 3% policy-rate world.
3. [claim-3] The **competitive bull case is overstated**: USDC winning “adjusted transaction volume” is not the same as winning economics, while USDT still owns the larger and stickier supply base; meanwhile, Circle’s supposed diversification into Mint, payments, and enterprise rails remains too small and too unproven to offset margin compression in the core business.

## Confidence
- claim-1: High
- claim-2: High
- claim-3: Medium

## Uncertainties
- A stronger-than-expected regulatory moat from MiCA and the GENIUS Act could justify a higher multiple than a pure financial utility would normally receive.
- If Circle proves that fee revenue can scale quickly through Visa, Intuit, cross-border settlement, or enterprise APIs, the bear case weakens materially.
- A renewed crypto bull cycle or further institutional adoption of USDC could keep valuation elevated longer than fundamentals alone would justify.
