## Mind Changes
- Q1: No change. The market should not pay a premium for capital throughput that does not yet produce material direct fees. Circle gets paid on **balances and yield**, not on token velocity. Until USDC’s adjusted volume shows up as disclosed, recurring fee revenue, Tether’s larger, stickier supply base is economically more valuable than Circle’s prettier flow statistic.
- Q2: No credible near-term bridge has been shown. If a 100 bp cut strips roughly **$810M** of reserve income off an **$81B** base, Circle needs either enormous net-new monetized volume or much fatter software pricing than anyone has demonstrated. Visa and Stripe are not going to leave Circle fat economics. They own distribution and customer relationships.
- Q3: I’ll concede one point: the regulatory moat probably gives Circle a floor, so this may not implode overnight. But that still does not justify buying a spread business at roughly **17x sales** right into an earnings trough. If you believe the long-term network story, the rational move is to wait until margin compression and OpEx expansion flush out the fantasy multiple.

## Strongest Remaining Claim
- `claim-1`: Circle is materially overvalued. That is still the cleanest surviving thesis. Q1: unmonetized volume is not earnings power. Q2: the fee-replacement math is brutal and likely multi-year, not a couple of quarters. Q3: paying a software multiple before the company proves software economics is how investors end up owning a money market fund cosplaying as fintech. At **$114-$132**, the stock still prices optionality as if it were already earned.

## Others' Blind Spots
- Claude’s blind spot is confusing **usage intensity** with **pricing power**. He keeps treating adjusted transaction volume like a toll road when Circle has not shown the toll schedule.
- Gemini’s blind spot is intellectual inconsistency. His math is bearish, his framework is bearish, his catalyst timing is bearish, and then he lands on `HOLD` because the compliance story sounds important. That is not discipline.
- Both of them underweight the commodity nature of stablecoins. Compliance matters, yes. It does not automatically create durable margins when banks, fintechs, and other issuers can all compete for the same regulated dollar flows.
