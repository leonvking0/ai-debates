1. **On Volume vs. Supply Economics:** Codex argues that because Circle's current revenue relies heavily on reserve yield, Tether's larger, stickier supply base ($184B) is economically superior to Circle's high-velocity institutional volume ($2.2T). If USDC's "adjusted transaction volume" currently generates zero direct fees, why should the market pay a premium for capital throughput rather than persistent, yield-generating deposit balances?

2. **On the Mathematics of the Fee Pivot:** Gemini calculates that offsetting just a 100-basis-point Fed rate cut (roughly $810M in lost 100%-margin yield) would require $810 billion in net-new payment volume, assuming an optimistic 10 bps net take-rate. Given the massive pricing power of enterprise partners like Visa and Stripe, what is the realistic timeline and mathematical path for Circle's non-interest software and API fees to replace this evaporating top-line revenue?

3. **On the Valuation Multiple During the "Trough":** Claude admits that near-term earnings will face pressure from Fed rate cuts, while Gemini and Codex stress that a ~17x Price-to-Sales multiple is indefensible for a contracting spread business. Even accepting the bullish premise that Circle successfully transitions into a Visa-like payment network long-term, how can investors justify buying at a $30B valuation today rather than waiting out the impending period of severe margin compression and OpEx expansion?
